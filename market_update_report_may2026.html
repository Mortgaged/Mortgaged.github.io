01 — Interest Rates

Bank of England Holds at 3.75%, But Warning Signs Grow

The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee voted 8–1 on 30 April to hold the base rate at 3.75% for the third consecutive meeting. One member voted to increase to 4%, and the Bank's own language pointed to the possibility of "forceful" rate rises later in 2026 if inflation continues to rise above its 2% target. CPI inflation currently stands at 3.3%.

For buyers and sellers, the important distinction is between the base rate and the rates lenders are actually offering. Fixed mortgage rates are driven primarily by swap rates, which have been pushed higher by global energy market pressures linked to the Middle East conflict, meaning most borrowers are seeing rates closer to 5% than 3.75%.

02 — Market Sentiment

What People Are Talking About

Market conversation this fortnight is dominated by three overlapping themes: swap rate volatility driven by the Middle East conflict, the prospects for a rate cut vs. rate rise at June's MPC meeting, and how elevated mortgage pricing is filtering into buyer confidence locally.

Major lenders including Nationwide, HSBC, Halifax and Santander have been cutting fixed rates in recent weeks, but experts are cautioning this may reverse if swap rates continue to climb. Buyers are being advised to lock in an affordable deal now and review before completion, rather than wait for a cleaner signal that may not arrive.

With around 1.8 million fixed-rate mortgages due to mature in 2026, there is a significant volume of remortgage decisions in play nationally, creating both urgency and opportunity for well-positioned buyers and sellers.

03 — Two-Month Outlook

Where Is the Market Heading?

The next MPC decision on 18 June is the pivotal moment. Most economists expect the base rate to hold again, but the Bank's own modelling now includes scenarios where the base rate rises to 5.25% by early 2027 if the energy price shock persists. Goldman Sachs sees a "low hurdle" for two hikes during the summer should energy pressures continue.

For fixed rate mortgage pricing, the near-term outlook depends almost entirely on swap rate movement rather than the base rate itself. If the Middle East situation stabilises, cuts in fixed rates could resume. If it escalates, the recent improvements could be erased. Forecasters currently project 5-year fixes in the 4.73% range by end of May, edging toward 4.67% into June.

The practical advice from brokers is consistent: well-qualified buyers should proceed rather than wait. Sellers who price correctly, particularly in family home segments, are still achieving strong results. The key risk for sellers is overpricing in a market where buyers have more choice than at any point in the last eight years.

05 — Sale Price Achievement

Are Properties Achieving Asking Price?

Nationally, the average home now sells for approximately 4% below its original asking price, a gap that is slightly larger than this time last year. In the South West specifically, the average discount from asking price sits at around 2.86%, above the national average.

However, Bristol is the standout exception within the South West. Zoopla data highlights Bristol (BS) as the region's top performer, with an average sale time of just 22 days and minimal asking price reductions required. Well-priced family houses in South Bristol are seeing competitive demand, with some sellers achieving or exceeding asking price.

For estate agents advising vendors, the consistent message from the data is that realistic, evidence-based pricing at launch drives significantly better outcomes than aspirational pricing followed by reductions. Buyers in 2026 have more choice than at any point in eight years and are taking longer to commit where value is unclear.

06 — Key Talking Points for Your Clients

Practical Guidance This Fortnight

For buyers: Don't wait for the "perfect" rate. Lock in a competitive deal now, as most lenders will allow a rate review before completion if pricing improves. The SVR you fall onto at the end of a fixed deal (~7–8%) is significantly more expensive than any current fixed option.

For sellers in Bristol/Portishead/Nailsea: Houses, particularly terraced and semi-detached family homes, remain in demand. Accurate pricing at launch avoids the stigma of a visible price reduction. Flats require careful positioning given the wider market headwinds.

For remortgagors: Start the process up to 6 months before your deal ends. Around 1.8 million UK fixed-rate mortgages expire in 2026. Those who secure a rate offer early have options; those who drift onto SVR do not.

Watch: The June MPC meeting (18 June), May inflation data (due before June decision), and any shift in Middle East energy prices. These three factors will largely determine the direction of fixed rates into summer.
